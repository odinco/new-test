Things to try

  • Stop just past the 100% hole. The first loop is drawn. Keep pulling and the second loop forms on the other side of null.
  • Stretch the wear scar past the coil spacing. The differential splits into two small signals at the scar's ends while the absolute stays one hump.
  • Park coil A under the 60% hole. The eddy currents can't cross the hole, so they crowd through the thin ligament under it and glow white.
  • Drop to 25 kHz and pull past the ID groove and the support ring. The ID groove nearly disappears and the support takes over the screen.

  • Lower frequency goes deeper. Standard depth of penetration grows as 1/√frequency, so the field reaches the OD and past it.
  • Deeper field, less phase spread. Phase lag builds with depth measured in penetration depths. When the whole wall is a small fraction of one penetration depth, flaws at different depths land at similar angles and the curve bunches up.
  • High frequency favors the ID. Current crowds the inside surface, so ID flaws read strong and the angles spread out for sizing.
  • Low frequency favors what's outside. Supports, deposits and OD conditions grow, which is why the low channel is for locating supports and feeding the mix, not sizing.
