CORESTAR ANALYSIS

SETUP GUIDE

Six steps to build a setup you can trust before you look at a single tube.

1
Load the data
Open the exam data and the calibration file. Confirm the probe type, frequencies, and tube range match the inspection plan.
CHECK Cal standard ID matches the data sheet
2
Set up channels
Assign differential and absolute channels for each frequency. Build any required mixes (for example, support suppression) per your procedure.
CHECK Every channel is labeled and in the right order
3
Set the phase
On the cal standard, rotate so probe motion is horizontal. Set the through-wall hole signal to the angle your procedure calls for.
CHECK Signal travels in the expected direction
4
Set the voltage
Normalize the reference signal (for example, the 4 × 20% flat-bottom holes) to the voltage in your procedure. Apply it to all channels.
CHECK Reference reads the target voltage
5
Build the curve
Mark each cal flaw (100%, 60%, 40%, 20%) and save the phase-to-depth curve. Points should form a smooth line.
CHECK No point sits far off the curve
6
Save & verify
Save the setup with a clear name. Open a known tube and confirm supports and landmarks look normal before you start.
CHECK Setup name includes unit, probe & date
QUICK
TIPS
Change one setting at a time and re-check the cal standard after each change.
Use the same setup for every tube in the same exam group.
If a step won't line up, stop and ask. Don't force it.

HOW TO ANALYZE

A repeatable routine for every tube: scan, spot, measure, size, code, and report.

1
Scan the tube
Scroll the strip chart along the full length of the tube. Find the landmarks first: tube sheet, supports, and U-bend.
CHECK All expected landmarks are present
2
Spot signals
Look for anything that breaks the normal background or looks different from a typical support signal.
CHECK Review the primary channel first, then the others
3
Read the Lissajous
Box the signal and look at its shape in the Lissajous window. Measure the peak-to-peak voltage and note the phase angle.
CHECK Signal is clean, not noise or probe wobble
4
Size it
Read the depth from your calibration curve using the phase angle. Confirm the call on a second frequency or mix.
CHECK Both frequencies tell the same story
5
Code & locate
Give it the right report code (for example, % depth, DNT, or NDD). Record the location in inches from the nearest landmark.
CHECK Code comes from the approved code list
6
Report & review
Enter the result, save, and move to the next tube. Flag anything unusual for a second analyst or resolution review.
CHECK Every tube has a result, even if it's NDD
QUICK
TIPS
When unsure, compare the signal to the same spot on a clean tube.
Always measure location from the nearest landmark, not the tube end.
If you can't call it with confidence, flag it. Don't guess.

INDICATION FIELD GUIDE

The flaws we see most, which channel to call them on, and what to watch for.

IDI

ID pitting

CH1 · M1 on supports
Looks like
Small, sharp, clean figure-8. Angle sits on the ID side of the through-wall line.
Tell-tale
Strongest on the highest frequency, since CH1 is most sensitive at the inside surface.
Watch out
Probe noise, scratches, and ID deposits. Confirm it repeats on another channel.
ODI

OD pitting

CH1 · M1 on supports
Looks like
Figure-8 rotated past the through-wall line toward OD. Read depth from the cal curve.
Tell-tale
Also shows on lower frequencies. Shallow OD pits can be weak on CH1 but clear on CH3/CH5.
Watch out
Support edges and deposits pull the angle. Very low frequency drift alone is not an ODI.
WAR

Wear

M2
Looks like
A broad, slow signal right at a support or AVB once the mix removes the support.
Tell-tale
Always at a support edge. Often shows on neighboring tubes in high-flow areas.
Watch out
Normal mix residual can look like wear. Compare with clean supports in the same tube.
ERO

Erosion

CH6 (absolute)
Looks like
A gradual drift on the absolute strip chart over a length of tube, not a sharp figure-8.
Tell-tale
Common at the inlet end, just past the tube sheet. Differentials may barely see it.
Watch out
Probe drift and the tube sheet transition. Compare with a clean stretch of the same tube.
DNT

Dent

CH1 · M1 on supports
Looks like
Large signal, lying close to the probe-motion direction. A geometry change, not wall loss.
Tell-tale
High voltage with no flaw-like angle. Often at supports where corrosion squeezes the tube.
Watch out
A dent can hide a flaw. Check inside it on the higher frequencies and the mix.
+

Other codes to know

Use your approved code list
NDDNo detectable degradation. Every clean tube still gets a result.
RSTRestriction. The probe won't pass, so record where it stopped.
PLGPlugged tube. Not tested, but still recorded.
DSIDistorted support signal. Flag it for a closer look.

Codes vary by site. Swap these for the ones in your procedure.

QUICK
TIPS
At a support, switch to the mix before you call anything.
Call on the listed channel, then confirm on one other.
Not sure what it is? Flag it. Don't force a code.
